# Literature notes and research directions

Updated 18 November 2026. These are paraphrased research notes with locators and limits, followed by proposed game implications. Proposals are not historical parameter estimates.
The [BibTeX bibliography](../references/research.bib) includes the earlier curated collection and these additions; [access notes](../references/ACCESS.md) distinguish retrieval from reading.

## Storage at Tambo Viejo

### Valdez and Bettcher (2025): systems, not just coca

Lidio M. Valdez and Katrina J. Bettcher, *Los sistemas de almacenamiento inka de Tambo Viejo*, Boletín de Arqueología PUCP 36:1–19.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18800/boletindearqueologiapucp.202501.001) · [Publisher and open PDF](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/boletindearqueologia/article/view/29942).
Reading: all 19 pages of the supplied article; publisher metadata checked.

Tambo Viejo was an imperial center in the coastal Acarí Valley. The study documents six semisubterranean cists and storage in ceramic vessels, expanding the architectural picture beyond large highland qollqa complexes.
Maize, coca, and other plant remains support storage interpretations, with differing strength across contexts; some cists were empty.
Two pieces of white chuño in a disturbed vessel suggest processed highland food reached the coast. This is an exchange connection, not evidence that coastal conditions enabled local freeze-drying.

Useful numerical locators:

| Context | Reported estimate | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Cist 2, printed p. 6 | About 2.1 cubic meters, equivalent to roughly 1,260 kg of dry maize | Capacity conversion, not an observed filled stock |
| Cist 5, printed p. 11 | About 1.8 cubic meters; alternative coca estimates around 540 or 760 kg | Conversion depends on the comparative assumptions |

These estimates describe physical capacity rather than annual food throughput.
Proposed environmental explanations for preservation do not constitute controlled measurements of annual loss, internal temperature, or humidity at the site.
Nor does imperial storage at this coastal center establish unrestricted household access or the size of a highland household reserve.

**Game use:** distinguish facility, commodity, stock, and withdrawal rights. Use regional examples in explanatory notes. Do not assign a universal numerical storage bonus from this study.

### Valdez (2026 issue): a separate coca article

Lidio M. Valdez, *El almacenamiento inka de las hojas de coca en Tambo Viejo, Perú*, Latin American Antiquity 37:127–133.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/laq.2025.10129).
Reading: supplied article text. The PDF has a 2026 issue heading, 2025 copyright, and an update notice; retain the DOI and check the current publisher version when citing.

This narrower report concerns coca leaves and seeds associated with clean sand in a small semisubterranean structure.
It complements the 2025 systems article. It is neither that article nor Morris's 1986 storage chapter.
Its preservation evidence does not calibrate staple-food calorie reserves.

## Negotiation, tribute, and mita

### A book presentation is a distinct source

Paula C. Zagalsky, *Obedecer, negociar y resistir: Tributo y mita indígena en Potosí, siglos XVI y XVII* (IEP and BCRP, 2023).
[Publisher summary and contents](https://fondoeditorial.iep.org.pe/producto/obedecer-negociar-y-resistir-tributo-y-mita-indigena-en-potosi-siglos-xvi-y-xvii/).
**The book itself has not been read for this contribution.** Its publisher summary concerns Visisa strategies in the Qaraqara federation; it is not an all-Andes quantitative model.

The [Museo Central presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z4qBcpoI4Zs) was read through the supplied timestamped transcript. Most substantive discussion is by commentators Fernando Jumar and Marco Curatola; Zagalsky's closing remarks focus mainly on research and acknowledgments.
The following are paraphrases of the presentation, not quotations or verified page claims from the book. Transcript errors remain possible.

| Time | Point discussed | Proposed game implication |
|---|---|---|
| 29:40–31:27 | Land and resettlement involved negotiation and alliances | Allow local responses rather than only a uniform yield penalty |
| 31:49–33:17 | Revisitas could reduce tribute assessments while mining obligations were more rigid | Separate tribute reassessment from mita quotas |
| 32:07–32:48 | Recorded population changes could reflect death, concealment, and negotiation | Separate registered tributaries from resident population |
| 33:24–34:02 | Cash substitution for labor, including payments that did not necessarily produce a replacement worker | Distinguish draft liability, substitution payment, and actual labor delivered |
| 34:10–35:02 | Proximity to Potosí could create work, trade, and evasion options | Consider distance as affecting both burden and opportunity |

Use these mechanisms within the case's scope, especially the late-sixteenth- and early-seventeenth-century Visisa/Qaraqara setting.
They justify questions and optional mechanics; they provide no calibrated negotiation probability, uniform commuting cost, or general wage-to-food conversion.
The author's mention of a doctoral-thesis basis suggests another lawful access route, still to be located and checked.

## Farming and exchange beyond a single mechanism

**Hirth and Pillsbury (2013),** [Redistribution and Markets in Andean South America](https://doi.org/10.1086/673114), Current Anthropology 54(5):642–647.
The supplied text's opening argument, pp. 642–643, was checked directly: centralized noncommercial redistribution alone does not describe the diversity of Andean economies; commercial and noncommercial exchange could coexist.
This supports a limited exchange option or a classroom question about omitted markets. It does not establish frictionless universal markets.

**Bruno (2014),** [Beyond Raised Fields](https://doi.org/10.1111/aman.12066), American Anthropologist 116(1):130–145.
Relevant discussion and conclusions in the supplied text were read, especially the Taraco farming synthesis on pp. 139–141.
The account includes crop and herd diversification, tillage, weeding, fertilization, and changes in land use rather than making raised fields the whole agricultural story.
This is Formative-period evidence, long before the game. It supports considering multiple farming practices, not transferring its chronology or inventing measured colonial yield bonuses.

## GDP and nutrition: define the quantity before calibrating

The official [MPD2023 release](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/historicaldevelopment/maddison/releases/maddison-project-database-2023) and [dataset record](https://doi.org/10.34894/INZBF2) identify the data and its source requirements.
The locally retrieved official workbook's Peru GDP-per-person entries were checked:

| Year | GDP per person, 2011 international dollars |
|---|---:|
| 1605 | 910 |
| 1610 | 1,076 |
| 1615 | 1,028 |
| 1620 | 995 |
| 1625 | 998 |
| 1630 | 1,046 |
| 1635 | 1,202 |

These are database reconstructions, not direct observations of ayllu output. The underlying colonial reconstruction's Peru/Bolivia scope also needs to be retained when interpreting the modern country label.
They do not validate the game's 1500–1600 path or convert Maddison 2003's 1990-dollar levels.
Cite the database and the underlying reconstruction, including Arroyo Abad and van Zanden (2016), when plotting this small country subset.

Nutrition work should keep separate: food produced, food available after obligations and exchange, wage purchasing power, actual intake, and dietary/health indicators.
Wernke and Whitmore (2009) models household production and requirements; Dell (2010) measures much later outcomes; Allen (2001) supplies a European real-wage method; isotopic patterns indicate dietary composition rather than exact daily calories.
None independently establishes the game's 1,700-to-1,200 kcal transition.
A household deficit fraction is also not the same parameter as a 30% dispersion around mean availability.

## Search order

1. Read Zagalsky's book or locate its thesis to test the distinction between tribute reassessment, draft quotas, substitution payments, and labor actually supplied.
2. Read highland storage studies for preservation and maintenance, and institutional studies for access and allocation.
3. Reconstruct household food budgets with seed retention, processing yields, transport, market exchange, and seasonal demand before tuning calorie endpoints.
4. Read resettlement case studies for local timing and negotiation rather than imposing one uniform trajectory.
5. Keep quipu scholarship and historical-game pedagogy as separate reading tasks: a legible game representation is not itself proof of historical recording practice.

Privately circulated teaching materials are not reproduced or linked here. Public bibliography and reading notes do not transfer redistribution rights in the underlying books, articles, or videos.

## Four supplied papers added to this contribution

### Urton and Chu (2015): accounting as a process

*Accounting in the King's Storehouse: The Inkawasi Khipu Archive*, Latin American Antiquity 26(4):512–529.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.7183/1045-6635.26.4.512).
Read: opening context and interpretation on printed pp. 523–527, including the numerical examples and discussion of accounting units.

The paper reports an archive of 34 khipus at Inkawasi in the Cañete Valley. Associations with chili peppers, peanuts, and black beans connect records with the storage setting, although the precise identification of each recorded commodity remains inferential.
Linked pairs and closely matching numbers are interpreted as checking and reconciling accounts. Addition and subtraction can relate the same quantities in different record arrangements.
The proposed use of rope-impressed floor grids for standardized produce units is a hypothesis, not a recovered universal unit-to-kilogram conversion.

**Game implication:** make the harvest quipu part of an accounting exercise. Students can reconcile incoming produce, a deduction, and the remaining stock, or compare two records containing an inconsistency. Separate observed numerical structure from inferred commodity labels.
This is a basis for a new learning activity; it does not authenticate the game's exact cord layout or establish that historical khipus recorded calories, GDP, or its welfare index.

### Urton and Chu (2019): a tax hypothesis with an explicit alternative

*The Invention of Taxation in the Inka Empire*, Latin American Antiquity 30(1):1–16.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/laq.2018.64).
Read: abstract, printed pp. 9–14, especially Tables 1–3, the alternative explanation, and conclusions.

The authors identify recurring fixed deductions of 10, 15, 47, and 208 accounting units within sequences of the form total = deduction + remainder(s).
They interpret these as provisions supporting storehouse personnel and operations. They also discuss seed retention as an alternative explanation, preferring the maintenance-tax interpretation while acknowledging uncertainty.
Some numerical sequences do not reconcile exactly; the meaning of the units and product associations cannot simply be assumed.

The reported approximate 2% assessment in three analyzed accounts is a relationship to average deposit size, with an approximately 11.2% outlier. It is not proof that every individual deposit or every Inka community paid a uniform 2% tax.
The article concerns proposed deductions at a state facility, not a demonstrated general tax on household produce.

**Game implication:** maintenance and seed retention are distinct possible reserve uses. A classroom task could ask students to compare those explanations of one ledger and identify what additional evidence would distinguish them.
Do not substitute the paper's fixed values or percentages for the game's community levy.

### Klaus and Tam (2009): health is not reducible to calorie totals

*Contact in the Andes: Bioarchaeology of Systemic Stress in Colonial Mórrope, Peru*, American Journal of Physical Anthropology 138:356–368.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.20944).
Read: abstract, local context, and discussion/conclusion on printed pp. 363–365.

This is the **2009 systemic-stress study**, separate from the registry's **2010 oral-health paper** by the same authors.
The coastal Mórrope comparison indicates elevated postcontact stress across several skeletal and demographic proxies, while adult stature was unchanged.
Lower enamel-hypoplasia prevalence did not straightforwardly indicate better health; the authors consider whether severe childhood disease killed children before such lesions formed.
Their explanation combines settlement aggregation, infection, labor, resource access, and environmental conditions. It does not isolate calories as the single cause.

**Game implication:** the present calorie/storage/resilience index should not be presented as a measurement of total historical welfare. Pair it with a qualitative health discussion, or explore infection and settlement density as separately labelled mechanisms.
Do not infer an exact kcal/day decline or transfer local skeletal ratios into annual mortality probabilities for the highland game.

### McCall (2016): critique the simulation, including its apparent success

*Teaching History With Digital Historical Games: An Introduction to the Field and Best Practices*, Simulation & Gaming 47(4):517–542.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1046878116646693).
Read: supplied advance-version PDF pp. 8–20. This PDF uses pages 1–26; the issue citation uses 517–542.

McCall discusses choice, replay, and counterfactuals alongside biases toward quantification, clear objectives, and exaggerated player information and control.
The practical recommendations emphasize reflective play logs, teacher facilitation, comparison with other historical sources, and discussion during and after play.
This is guidance for designing a lesson, not a demonstration that this particular game improves learning.

**Proposed class sequence:**

1. Predict a mechanism and explain the evidence behind the prediction.
2. Play a shared seed and record one decision and its expected consequence.
3. Replay that seed after changing one decision.
4. Compare the ledger with a historical source; identify an omitted mechanism or uncertain inference.
5. Explain which conclusion follows from the game and which would require additional historical evidence.

Assess the quality of the explanation and source comparison, rather than requiring one GDP-direction answer for every seed.
A strong extension combines the Urton accounts with this debrief: students explain why a reconciled numerical record can still have an uncertain historical interpretation.
