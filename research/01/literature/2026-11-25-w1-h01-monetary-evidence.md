# W1-H01 — Historical money-changing evidence audit (c. 100 CE)

- **Game:** *Silk Road Expedition Simulator (Han–Roman Era)* ([`../../../projects/01/index.html`](../../../projects/01/index.html))
- **Target period & geography:** c. 100 CE across Han China (`Chang'an`, `Dunhuang`), the Tarim Basin & Sogdiana (`Kashgar`, `Samarkand`), Parthia (`Merv`), and the Roman Levant & Italy (`Antioch`, `Rome`).
- **Reading status:** **Secondary search-summary read**; full primary editions (Dunhuang/Juyan Han wooden slips, Roman legal texts on *argentarii*, and numismatic metallurgical corpora) have not yet been inspected in full.

## Audit of game monetary claims and abstractions

| Game Label / Parameter | Classification | What Evidence Supports | Gap & Permitted Wording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Gold Florins` (`rateToFlorin`) | **Model assumption (anachronistic)** | The Florentine *fiorino d'oro* (~3.53 g, ~.986 fine gold) was first minted in **1252 CE**, over a millennium after c. 100 CE. | Must be explicitly labeled as a **synthetic classroom accounting unit**, not a historical Han–Roman coin. |
| `Chang'an`: `Chinese Copper Cash (Wen)` & `Dunhuang`: `Hexi Frontier Cash` | **Numismatically grounded (*wuzhu*), simplified at frontier** | Han China (202 BCE – 220 CE) used bronze *wuzhu* (五銖, introduced 118 BCE, ~3.25 g). Han wooden/bamboo slips from Dunhuang and Juyan attest a **dual economy**: *wuzhu* coins for garrison/local pay alongside **standardized bolts of silk (*pi*)** as high-value frontier currency. | Splitting Dunhuang into `"Hexi Frontier Cash"` at `9.5:1` vs. Chang'an `10:1` is a **model assumption**, not an attested separate coinage. |
| `Kashgar` & `Samarkand`: `Sogdian Silver Dirhams` | **Anachronistic label** | *Dirham* is an Arabic/Islamic-era coin name (7th century CE onward, derived from Greek *drachma*). In the 1st–2nd centuries CE, Tarim oases (notably Khotan) struck **Sino-Kharoshthi "horse coins"** linking Chinese *zhu* weight units and Kharoshthi legends, while Sogdiana struck **local/imitative silver coins** alongside Kushan and Parthian issues. | Rename or qualify in teaching notes as **"Central Asian / Sogdian & Tarim coinages (game abstraction)"**; do not claim *dirhams* existed in 100 CE. |
| `Merv`: `Parthian Silver Drachmas` | **Numismatically well-founded** | Merv (Margiana) was an eastern Arsacid mint center, and the **Parthian silver *drachm*** (~4 g, modified Attic standard) was the core Arsacid silver coin (247 BCE – 224 CE). | Supported as a historical coin type; its `1.5:1` rate remains a **model assumption**. |
| `Antioch` & `Rome`: `Roman Denarii` | **Numismatically well-founded (with regional nuance)** | Under Trajan (98–117 CE), the silver *denarius* (~3.41 g theoretical; ~93.5% silver before 107 CE, ~89% after 107 CE) was the imperial silver backbone. However, **Antioch** also struck civic bronze and silver **tetradrachms** on Greek standards. | Note that Roman Syria was a **multi-currency zone** (not *denarius*-only), and the `1:1` Antioch–Rome parity is a **model simplification**. |
| Money changers, `5%` fee & fixed rates (`10`, `9.5`, `2`, `1.5`, `1`) | **Institution attested; rates & `5%` fee are model assumptions** | In the Roman world, *argentarii* (bankers/money changers) and *nummularii* (assayers testing weight, purity, and plated *fourrées*) charged an exchange agio (*collybus*). | No historical source supplies Week 1's `5%` flat fee or `10:1`–`1:1` exchange table; present them as **synthetic teaching parameters**. |
