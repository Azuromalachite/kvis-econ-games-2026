## A separate financing comparison

Duarte Barbosa's early-sixteenth-century account, Henry E. J. Stanley translation, *A Description of the Coasts of East Africa and Malabar* (1866), section **“Brabares … merchants … Malabar”**, describes inland traders collecting pepper and ginger from cultivators, sometimes buying in advance in exchange for imported cotton cloth and other goods. [Section read in this digital transcription](https://www.victoriainstitutions.com/books/read.php?book=barbosa). This directly supports the existence of advance procurement in that account; it does not establish a cash loan, universal prepayment, exact ownership transfer, a Calicut sailing merchant's contract, or a percentage advance. Original Portuguese and the manuscript tradition were not checked. The transcription lacks reliable printed-page markers; its bracketed numbers are notes, not pages.

Sebastian R. Prange, **“‘Measuring by the bushel’: reweighing the Indian Ocean pepper trade,”** *Historical Research* 84(224), 2011, 212–235, [DOI 10.1111/j.1468-2281.2010.00547.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2281.2010.00547.x), selected pp. 215–219 and notes read in a copy (link not reproduced here; the DOI is the publisher's page). His pp. 217–218 connect intermediaries and advances; the June–September and October–November windows rest on Kieniewicz p. 21, not on a verified medieval contract. Footnote 30 identifies the relevant Barbosa passage as **Dames II, p. 56**; Stanley is a different translation. Prange's p. 216 calls Barbosa early fifteenth-century, an apparent century error: the account belongs to the early sixteenth century. Do not propagate that wording. Prange's Ben Yiju example is **cardamom**, not pepper, and describes a disputed advance rather than a standard settlement lag. It cannot calibrate the registered delay arm.

## Relationship to retained evidence

- De Simone and Trinco's ninth–fourteenth-century Kerala evidence identifies particular merchant affiliations and institutions. It does **not** identify Ma Huan's collectors as Jain, establish a Wayanad-to-Calicut shipment, or prove a named merchant controlled the entire chain. See the [earlier bounded findings](p02-p03-extended-search-addendum.md#23-november-follow-up-chronology-account-vessel-and-kerala).
- The existing [Chaudhuri chapters 9–10 reading note](2026-11-24-chaudhuri-ch9-ch10-reading-note.md) already separates spot and forward markets, trader finance, freight, and later coffee evidence. Do not duplicate that review here. These readings followed registration of the [depth/liquidity protocol](../pre-registrations/2026-11-23-depth-liquidity.md); none changes its hypothetical values or predictions.
- **Model implication, an inference:** distinguish obtaining stock, selling stock, and receiving spendable proceeds. Inland crop finance and a port buyer's spot purchase can coexist. No evidence here warrants imposing the same settlement delay on every good or port.
