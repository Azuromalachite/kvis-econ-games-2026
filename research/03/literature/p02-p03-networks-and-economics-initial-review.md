# Monsoon Navigator — P02 merchant networks and P03 transport economics

[Research index](README.md) · [Current document requests](document-requests.md)

Exploratory literature and documentary review, 22 November 2026.

## Research decision

Pursue P02 and P03 together through a bounded question: **What did distant agents and local institutions enable a merchant to do, and how did those arrangements affect costs, liquidity, and exposure to loss?**

This is more directly observable in the accessible documents than the causal proposition that seasonal residence created trust. P02 asks who performed services and how obligations were supported. P03 asks whose goods, capital, charges, and claims were involved. The same account can illuminate both without making a network tie equivalent to friendship or an accounting credit equivalent to profit.

The current [repository roadmap](https://github.com/Antooooon125/Week3-monsoon-navigator/blob/main/docs/research-directions-and-milestones.md) was checked directly: P02 concerns merchant networks/institutions; P03 concerns transport economics under information and capital constraints. The [baseline](https://github.com/Antooooon125/Week3-monsoon-navigator/blob/main/docs/baseline.md) was also read, blob `c97da72e60c4e52c10d3f4b37cc1851796e12b1d`. ## 1. P02: what the literature changes about the question

The relevant institutional literature is a disagreement to investigate, not a settled justification for a reputation meter.

| Research source | Argument in the passages read | Implication for our research |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Avner Greif, *The Maghribi traders: a reappraisal?* (2012), author long version [L1] | Defends multilateral reputation as a mechanism supporting agency. His response does not require complete exclusion of outsiders; isolated cross-group ties do not by themselves refute the model. | Test the scope of information and consequences of misconduct. Do not treat one cross-group transaction as a decisive falsifier. |
| Jeremy Edwards and Sheilagh Ogilvie, *Contract enforcement, institutions, and social capital* (2012) [L2] | Disputes the coalition interpretation and emphasizes a combination of legal enforcement and overlapping social relationships. | Code legal procedures and informal pressure separately; neither is an automatic residual category. |
| Jessica L. Goldberg, *Choosing and Enforcing Business Relationships* (2012), especially printed pp. 33–38 [L3] | Distinguishes reputations for diligence, competence, and connections from property protection through legal/market arrangements. Reciprocal services need not be equal tasks or hours. | Separate quality of service, influence, contractual rights, and accounting obligations. A single trust score may conceal different processes. |
| Phillip I. Ackerman-Lieberman, *The Business of Identity*, selected passages in chapters 2–3 [L4] | Challenges easy extrapolation from Jewish Geniza practice to all Islamic commerce; distinguishes partnership forms and examines mediation within legal institutions. | Record contract form and jurisdiction. Do not infer loss allocation from a generic label such as “partnership,” or assume mediation occurred outside the courts. |

These arguments primarily concern medieval Egypt and the Mediterranean. Their mechanisms are comparative leads for the Indian Ocean; they do not establish the same institutions at every game port or in every century. Finding a court case establishes that legal action was possible in that case, not that courts dominated all merchant relationships. Finding repeated exchange establishes continuity, not the cause of that continuity.

### A direct documentary anchor

PGP **5460**, a letter from Aden dated approximately 1130–1140, describes competing powers of attorney for people killed in a shipwreck. The claimants were to proceed to court in Fustat; the writer says the dispute could not be decided in Aden and obtained releases from responsibility. This supports a case study of representation, venue, information, and liability rather than an undifferentiated absence of law. Its catalogue names the sender Maḍmūn b. Yefet: do not silently merge him with the Maḍmūn b. Ḥasan named in other records. Relevant English translation: recto 2–18. [D3]

### Mechanisms to distinguish

| Mechanism | Evidence that would help identify it | Common misclassification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated service | Dated instructions, performance reports, later dealings between identified people | Every recurring name coded as trust |
| Expertise and workload | Requests for a specific skill; statements about inability to complete a task | Delayed service coded as dishonesty |
| Connections and political access | Named introduction, patron, exemption, or intervention | Any discount coded as the effect of friendship |
| Formal representation and enforcement | Power of attorney, witnessed account, release, claim, settlement, judgment | Mention of a dispute coded as successful enforcement |
| Kinship, language, communal affiliation | Explicit relationship or self-identification | Names used to assign fixed ethnic or religious behavior |
| Seasonal residence | Arrival/departure bounds, explicit reason for staying, relationship chronology | Correspondence date treated as duration of residence |

The coding should permit multiple mechanisms in one transaction. Preserve a distinction between advice, instruction, reported action, allegation, admission, and confirmed settlement.

## 2. P03: a tractable account, with limits

**PGP 5457**, inferred 1133–1140, records three copper bags distributed across three ships; the motive is unstated. Its disputed iron delivery uses different weight measures, preventing a simple lost-quantity subtraction. This is Maḍmūn b. Ḥasan's Aden account to Avraham Ben Yiju, copied from PGP 5427. [D1]

For the copper-related listed charges in recto 40–50:

| Component | Dinars as reported |
| --- | ---: |
| Purchase | 415 |
| Hides and packing | 1 1/2 |
| Exit tolls | 4 1/8 |
| Freight | 4 1/2 |
| **Calculated subtotal** | **425 1/8** |

The following 2-dinar registration charge concerns other goods and is excluded. This subtotal supplies neither complete cost nor profit. Conversions to game D or tonnes remain unresolved. [D1]

**PGP 5452**, dated 526 AH in the record, provides another account between Maḍmūn b. Ḥasan and Avraham Ibn Yiju. A copper shipment is explicitly exempted from freight. Elsewhere, the sender disputes the recipient's account and reports that imports from Zabīd arrived too late for departing ships. The reason for the exemption is not established by those lines. Useful locators are recto 24–29 and 38–47. The document supports heterogeneous charges, accounting disagreement, and supply timing; it does not identify a general discount rule, a probability of cheating, or an Aden–Calicut price spread. The record gives the year range 23 November 1131–11 November 1132 CE and describes the letter as Aden, 1132; preserve both forms. [D2]

### Financing needs contract-level evidence

PGP **2367**, Bilbays, September 1239, records an arrangement initially described as qirāḍ becoming a fixed monthly payment obligation and involving a house transfer with redemption provisions. The English translation distinguishes the stages, payments in dirhams, and principal/property valuation in dinars. It is a useful warning against treating profit sharing and debt as interchangeable. It is a later Egyptian comparison, not an Indian Ocean voyage contract or evidence for the game's interest rate. No annual percentage is inferred without a justified exchange rate and contract interpretation. Relevant translation: recto 7–14. [D7]

## 4. Corpus feasibility and reuse

The Geniza Lab describes an Indian Ocean documentary project covering approximately **1080–1240**, with **668 documents and counting** on its project page. That figure is the page's description, not a count reproduced here or a statement that all items have English translations. [C1]

The latest archived metadata release reached from Zenodo's version link is **v1.2, published 8 September 2026**, DOI [10.5281/zenodo.22663982](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22663982). The live pages inspected display PGP application v4.32.0. Dataset release and application version are different identifiers. The archive was located, not downloaded or audited. [C2]

The v1.2 metadata README was read directly at [the release](https://github.com/princetongenizalab/pgp-metadata/blob/v1.2/README.md), blob `7c24b2a7834ae56638c55b02041e772f54fe7410`. It specifies **CC BY-NC 4.0**, documents tables and joins, and says person/place exports omit the full document relationship links. It contains stale-looking statements about being a first release and earlier collection/software dates. Consequently its row counts should not be reported as independently verified v1.2 counts. [C3]

PGP also provides an automatically updated [text-export repository](https://github.com/princetongenizalab/pgp-text). Its README explains numeric directories and annotation backups, but does not establish blanket reuse permission for all translations or manuscript images. Preserve translator/editor attribution and check rights per material before republishing text or images. Software licensing is not a substitute for data or image rights. [C4]

For a later reproducible pilot: pin the metadata release, pin the text-export commit, save the retrieval date for live records, and retain the exact edition/translation attribution. Do not construct a complete merchant network from co-occurring names or exported document counts alone.

### Proposed pilot, not yet conducted

1. Assemble a bounded candidate pool from the Indian Ocean project, retaining untranslated and uncertain entries in a coverage table. Record all inclusion/exclusion decisions.
2. Use a 30–50-record feasibility sample with purposeful coverage of successful service, disputes, legal instruments, accounts, and proposed transactions. Label it purposive; do not estimate historical frequencies from it.
3. Maintain separate identifiers for physical fragment, document, copy/join, transaction, dispute series, person, and correspondence cluster. An accusation and a reply are linked observations of one dispute.
4. Code person identity conservatively. Keep possible aliases separate until a cited identity decision resolves them. Distinguish owner, financier, carrier, agent, consignee, witness, and official; a person may hold several roles.
5. Annotate residence bounds and whether they precede relationship formation. Missing residence evidence remains missing.
6. Obtain a second reader for a subset and report disagreements in identity, event type, translation, dates, and contract classification. This review has not had independent annotation.
7. Only then decide whether M5 can test a seasonal-residence hypothesis, or whether the evidence supports a narrower institutional case study.

## 5. P03 parameter identifiability

These classifications describe the evidence found in this review. “Unknown” means not constrained here; it does not establish that no relevant evidence exists.

| Quantity or mechanism | Evidence status | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Some itemized acquisition and shipment charges | Direct local observation in D1 | Reconstruct bounded accounts in their original units |
| Freight exemptions or differing terms | Direct occurrence in D2; cause unknown | Do not assume a universal zero charge or deterministic social discount |
| Agent services and delayed recovery of proceeds | Documentary occurrence; workload/reasons vary | Separate reported sale, receivable, and spendable cash |
| Contract-specific allocation of obligations | Direct case evidence and scholarly comparisons | Model contract terms explicitly; avoid one generic loan label |
| Common currency and mass scale across ports | Unresolved | Preserve denomination, quality, large/small measure, and conversion provenance |
| Historical route-specific buy/sell spreads for all six game goods | Not identified | Game price ratios remain design assumptions for this review |
| Dhow/junk payload, acquisition cost, depreciation, and full operating cost | Not identified | No historical estimate of which vessel is more profitable |
| Costs of living, storage, financing, and delayed opportunities while waiting | Mechanisms plausible; comparable rate data not extracted | Waiting's net benefit cannot yet be calibrated |
| Merchant knowledge of future destination prices | Not identified as perfect foresight | Treat an omniscient strategy as a model benchmark |
| Probability and dependence of loss across ships | Not identified | Split shipment does not establish independent risks or a loss probability |
| Every month's automatic standing gain and its discount thresholds | No numerical historical support found | Retain as explicit classroom rules pending evidence and review |

The inspected baseline awards **15 standing per month waited**, changes fees at standing thresholds, supplies fixed price schedules with a deterministic seasonal variation, and distinguishes 120-tonne and 300-tonne holds. These are code parameters, not measurements furnished by the literature above. The R0 policy comparisons were already exploratory and rankings depended on policy. No new sweep or independent reproduction was run in this review.

### Accounting framework for a later research model

Keep inventory, cash, receivables, liabilities, and ownership separate. A bookkeeping identity can organize missing information without filling it with invented constants:

**Net trading result = realized sales − acquisition − freight − handling/customs − operating/residence costs − finance costs − losses and settlement costs.**

Each term needs a definition that prevents double counting. For example, sale proceeds may already be net of an auctioneer's fee; recording the same fee again creates an artificial loss. An account credit can remain unavailable for purchasing the next cargo. An investor's share is not automatically the whole venture's profit. Unsold cargo needs an explicit terminal valuation rule.

For a vessel comparison, define the business first: owning a ship, chartering it, or buying freight space. A merchant's five-bahār consignment cannot identify the carrier's total capacity or vessel operating cost. More hold capacity helps only when financing, available cargo, demand, and time permit its use; this is a proposed model condition, not an empirical estimate from this sample.

Before new M3/M4/M6 simulations, preregister capital constraints, information available at each decision, financing terms, waiting costs, accounting definitions, horizon/terminal valuation, insolvency treatment, policy choices, uncertainty ranges, and random-seed pairing. Disclose the prior R0 look and the present exploratory sources. Report reversed rankings and inconclusive ranges.

## 6. Implications worth developing for the game

These are evidence-informed design hypotheses, not implemented mechanics or validated parameter recommendations.

| Candidate decision | Research connection | Smallest useful comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sail personally or entrust a consignment to an agent | Agency and representation cases | Identical goods/capital with different control, information delay, and settlement terms |
| Consolidate cargo or divide it between departures | Split shipment in D1 | Vary fees, timing, and correlated loss; do not assume splitting is always safer |
| Accept a quick sale or wait for goods/payment/information | D2 and the dispute series | Distinguish weather waiting from supply and settlement delay |
| Pay for a documented agreement or rely on an existing relationship | Institutional debate and legal records | Compare kinds of obligation and available remedies rather than a universal “trust versus law” choice |
| Select an agent for competence, connections, or availability | Goldberg's comparative interpretation | Make trade-offs visible without equating social importance with honesty |

Start with **one transaction dossier** and **one dispute dossier** as historical teaching material. They can show a merchant's constraints before adding more currencies, contracts, or meters to the interface. The source documents' Aden–Mangalore and Egypt contexts must remain visible; they should not be relabeled as direct evidence for Calicut, Malacca, or Quanzhou.
