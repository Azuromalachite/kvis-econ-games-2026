## What it supports for Monsoon Navigator

- **Storage and care costs:**
  - warehouse rent, 1½ dinar, and mats for the warehouse, ¼ dinar (line 26);
  - airing out textiles: 2 dinars for the burds and 1 for the fūṭas (line 27).
- **Transaction costs:**
  - customs (lines 18–21);
  - cargo registration fees: 1 dinar for the pepper and 1 dinar less ⅙ for the bales of cloth (line 28);
  - brokerage and weighing for the pepper (line 29);
  - polishing and packing coral, and its transport (lines 29–30).
- **Goods remaining at the time of the account:** two burds, 17 khamāsiyyas of musk, and the pepper "with your servant" (lines 34–35).
- "Carried over" (lines 33–34) refers to the **credit balance**, not to the goods.

## What it does not support

- **Rates.** There is no rate per unit, per month or per tonne. The account gives totals for one year's business, with no quantities or durations to divide by.
- **Market behaviour.** Nothing on market capacity, price impact, recovery of demand, or settlement delay.
- **Unsold goods.** It doesn't say why goods remained: unsold, held back, or awaiting shipment.
- **Generalisation.** It is one account by one agent in one port (Aden, 1130s). It describes neither the game's other ports nor its period.
- **Currency.** Dinar amounts can't be converted into the game's D.

## Discrepancies in the English translation

On the record as read, two numerals in the English translation disagree with both the Judaeo-Arabic transcription and the Hebrew translation:

| Line | English | Transcription | Hebrew |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 17 (total credits) | 1,496 dinars 17 qīrāṭs | "one thousand four hundred and sixty-nine", 17 qīrāṭs | 1,469 dinars 17 qīrāṭs |
| 33 (remaining to his credit) | 1,760 dinars 13 qīrāṭs | "one hundred sixty-seven", 13 qīrāṭs | 167 dinars 13 qīrāṭs |

**The arithmetic confirms the transcription.** With 24 qīrāṭs to the dinar, and the line 32 total of 1,302⅙ = 1,302 4/24:

- 1,469 17/24 − 1,302 4/24 = 167 13/24, the line 33 balance in the transcription and the Hebrew.
- With the English line 17 figure, 1,496 17/24 − 1,302 4/24 = 194 13/24, which matches neither 167 nor 1,760.

The English figures read as transcription slips. This affects no use of the source in this repository.

## Aden price examples

The market ledger uses Marina Rustow's English translation of the recto, lines 5–6
and 9, together with the record's description of the currency. The account gives
two pepper valuations of 36 and 37 Malikī dinars per bahār and credits 31 fūṭas
at 15 dinars and 19 qīrāṭs. These are observations from one merchant's account
in Aden around 1134. They supply no conversion to game D or paired Calicut price.

- Şevket Pamuk, [*Prices in the Ottoman Empire, 1469–1914*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/prices-in-the-ottoman-empire-14691914/E73150A57D16E1184E2EB33626E1B2E1),
  pp. 452–459 (relevant sections read), illustrates keeping commodity, currency
  and silver-content measures explicit. The Istanbul consumer index supplies no
  Week 3 cargo price.
- Duarte Barbosa, [*A Description of the Coasts of East Africa and Malabar*](https://www.victoriainstitutions.com/books/read.php?book=barbosa),
  Henry E. J. Stanley translation (1866), pepper passage (read), reports
  200–230 fanams per bahal in Calicut and lists export duty separately.
  This early-sixteenth-century quotation is a later comparison, outside the
  game's composite c. 1000–1450 setting.
