Winds and navigation

Monsoon
A seasonal shift in prevailing winds. In the game, the southwest monsoon blows from southwest toward northeast in June–September. The northeast monsoon blows in the opposite direction in November–February. National Weather Service
Heading and bearing
A heading is the direction the ship points. A route bearing is the compass direction toward the next port. North is 0°, east 90°, south 180°, and west 270°. The game uses the route bearing as the heading.
Tailwind, crosswind, and headwind
A tailwind blows mostly along your route. A crosswind blows across it. A headwind blows mostly against it and can slow, damage, or turn back your ship in the game.
Transition months and doldrums
March–May and October are transition months in the game, marked as a dead zone for sailing. You can still depart, but cyclones or doldrums, periods of weak wind that may leave a vessel becalmed, can make the voyage more costly.
Nautical mile (nm) and knot (kt)
A nautical mile measures distance at sea and equals 1.852 km. A knot is one nautical mile per hour. The game shows wind in knots and your estimated ship speed in nautical miles per day.
Transit time and forecast
Transit time is the estimated time at sea for one leg. The forecast lets you compare departure months without advancing the voyage. Only Sail now departs.

Ships, cargo, and crew

Dhow and lateen sail
A dhow is a traditional sailing vessel associated with the western Indian Ocean. A lateen sail is triangular and set on a long angled spar. The game’s dhow is the smaller vessel and costs less to crew.
Junk and watertight bulkhead
A junk is a traditional Chinese sailing vessel. A watertight bulkhead is a partition that limits flooding between compartments. The game’s junk carries more cargo and takes less hull damage. UNESCO
Hull integrity and careening
The hull is the ship’s outer body. Hull integrity measures its condition. Historically, careening meant tilting a vessel to expose its underside for work. In the game, the shipyard repair raises hull integrity.
Hold, payload, and tonnage
The hold is the space for cargo. Payload is how much weight the vessel can carry. Tonnage in the cargo panel is the weight of goods aboard, shown in tons.
Bulk, semi-bulk, and luxury goods
Bulk goods such as timber take up much more of your weight allowance per lot. The game labels pepper semi-bulk and cloves and ivory luxury goods. Compare the likely sale margin per ton as well as the margin per lot.
Crew wages, crew health, and mutiny
Wages are monthly crew pay. Sailing lowers crew health, and rest in port restores it. A mutiny is a revolt by the crew. Below 50% health, the game may trigger one at the end of a voyage and charge two months of wages to settle it.
Tarpaulin and jettison
A tarpaulin is a cover that protects cargo from rain and spray. To jettison cargo means to throw it overboard, usually to lighten the ship in an emergency.

Trade and the voyage ledger

Emporium, entrepôt, and port layover
An emporium is a major trading center. An entrepôt is a port where goods are brought in and sent on to other markets. A layover is time spent in port while waiting for the next departure.
Merchant diaspora and standing
A merchant diaspora is a network of traders who live away from their places of origin and maintain ties across ports. Standing is the game’s measure of your local relationships. Higher standing can lower port fees, tariffs, or piracy risk.
Tariff and dirham (D)
A tariff is a tax on traded goods. In the game it is added to your purchase price. The dirham is the game’s unit of money, shown as D.
Arbitrage and cost basis
Arbitrage means taking advantage of price differences, such as buying pepper where it is cheap and selling it at a higher-priced port. Cost basis in the game is the average amount paid per held lot, including tariffs.
Commenda and interest
A commenda was a medieval partnership in which an investor financed a trader’s venture, often for a share of the profit. The game simplifies this as a loan. Interest is the amount added to the debt every six months. The Commenda
Net profit and return on equity
Net profit is the money left after final cargo sales and debt settlement, minus your starting equity. Return on equity expresses that gain or loss as a percentage of starting equity.
Liquidation and audit
Liquidation turns leftover cargo into cash at the end of the campaign. The audit is the final record of your voyage, decisions, finances, and reflection.

    Historiography & Engine Equations

    Monsoon Vector Physics & Maritime Architecture

    T = Distance / [ V_base × max(0.25, cos(θ_route − θ_wind) × α_hull + 0.5) × 0.65 (transition months) × 1.15 (navigator) ]

    1. Sewn-Plank Dhow vs. Bulkhead Junk: Arab/Indian dhows stitched teak planks with coconut coir rope, allowing hulls to flex over coral reefs. Chinese junks used iron nails and watertight bulkheads. In the game, α only scales how much the wind along the route changes speed: the dhow (α = 0.85) gains more with a following wind and loses more against it, and both ships get the same factor in a pure crosswind. The junk takes half the hull damage from headwinds and cyclones and carries 300 tons.

    2. Monsoons and merchant communities (Chaudhuri & Abu-Lughod): The game models winds blowing from the southwest toward the northeast in Jun–Sep and from the northeast toward the southwest in Nov–Feb. Mar–May and Oct are transition months in this simulation. Seasonal layovers helped create opportunities for merchants to build relationships in port communities.
